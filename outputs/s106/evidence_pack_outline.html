S106 Night Quota renewal: our evidence pack

The Section 106 (Fourth Schedule) requires the night noise quota to be re-agreed every 5 years between the airport and BCP as local planning authority. If they disagree, an independent Specialist decides, weighing seven listed considerations. This outline maps our evidence to each one, so BCP can be armed before the airport's proposal lands.
Draft skeleton · owners are suggestions · verify all dates via the BCP records request

The mechanism and the moment

Every 5 years: the airport must propose the next quota to BCP at least 6 months before expiry, with reasoned justification. BCP may refuse and propose alternatives — including a lower quota. Unresolved disputes go to a Specialist bound by the seven considerations below. If nothing is agreed, the old quota stays in force, so pressing hard carries no risk. Our reading of the dates: current period likely runs to ~March 2028, making the airport's proposal due by ~September 2027 (to be confirmed by the records request to BCP, along with what happened at the 2013, 2018 and 2023 renewals).

(vii)Economic and social benefits of the Airport
In hand

This is the airport's ground — but it cuts our way: by its own published chart it used only ~9% of the quota in 2022 (~267 of 3,100 points), so a substantially lower quota causes zero harm to current operations. We are not against the airport or its reasonable growth.

To build

Estimate current usage (2023–25) from our data + the monitoring reports, to show even today's doubled night traffic sits far below the budget.

Suggested

Michael

The core argument: the airport uses a small fraction of a generous 2007 noise budget while night flights double underneath it and national policy tightens above it. A renewed quota set at, or below, actual current usage — paired with a Southampton-style movement cap and council monitoring — protects residents at no cost to today's operations. That is a proposal a reasonable LPA can adopt and a Specialist can defend on every one of the seven considerations.
