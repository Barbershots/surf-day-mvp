The problem

The situation is already unbearable for many, and it is getting worse.

We are not against aircraft, nor the expansion of Bournemouth Airport. But we are not alone in finding the rising noise a real concern, and we want to work with the authorities to reduce it. We believe that can be done without harming others, the airport, or its growth.

Many residents can no longer fully enjoy their homes and gardens. Conversations are interrupted indoors and out as aircraft pass over, and families are regularly woken by late-night flights. The impact reaches beyond residents, to tourists and campers and the wider tranquillity of the New Forest National Park.

Brockenhurst sits just under 10 nautical miles out, directly in line with the main runway. It is made worse by aircraft arriving lower than the airport's own published rules, and often turning and levelling off right over the village, which needs extra engine power and extra noise. The numbers rise every year, and it is getting worse at night.

What we can do

Can we do anything about this?
Yes, and the time is now.

Bournemouth Airport is redrawing its flight paths right now, through the formal CAA process. The new satellite-guided routes are hyper precise: instead of today's scattered flights, aircraft will follow the exact same line. If that line is drawn over Brockenhurst as it is today, it becomes a permanent, concentrated noise highway. That is the threat, and it is also the opportunity to move it.

What we are asking for

Three simple changes.

1

Continuous descent, and stay higher

Hold every arrival to a smooth, engine-idle glide at a steeper angle, so aircraft are higher and quieter over the village, with no levelling off.

→
2

Avoid Brockenhurst

Use the precision of the new routes to place the line away from the village, without shifting the burden onto other communities.

→
3

No fixed noise corridor

Keep the design simple, but do not lock every flight onto one line. Vary the routes so no single area carries all the impact.

The law is on our side. The airport has a Section 106 commitment to continuous descent, and a duty under the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 to further the National Park's purposes, putting conservation first.
