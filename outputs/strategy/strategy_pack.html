Working group · internal strategy · July 2026

Where this stands, who we need to move,
and what we do next

The meeting with Russell changed the picture. The airport is sympathetic but says it can change nothing for years, because Bournemouth's flight-path work has been absorbed into the national UKADS redesign. This pack sets out the timeline, the people who hold the levers, and a plan built around near-term pressure while the long process runs.

2026–2027

The interim window (where we have leverage)

Route changes are frozen, but procedures are not. Push complaints, the BCP / Section 106 route, noise monitoring, and renegotiation of the Solent handover. Watch that a new GPS route is not hard-wired over the village.

~2028

Public consultation (best estimate)

The formal CAP 1616 "Consult" stage, our main chance for resident input into the actual design. Everything we build now is to be ready and credible for this.

2032–2035

New airspace deployed (target)

The government's target window (the airport also cites 2033-35). Its position: no approved route changes before this. The redesign aims to route around Brockenhurst, so the fight is to protect that outcome and win interim relief in the meantime.

The strategic read: the "nothing until 2035" line only applies to the big structural redesign. Interim, local fixes (ATC handover agreements, night procedures, planning enforcement) can move in months. That gap is where BCAG concentrates its pressure.

Deliverable 3 · extension

Ideas not yet on the table

New angles beyond what the group has already discussed, each with a suggested owner to get it moving. All are for discussion, not decisions.

1Use the Environmental Information Regulations, not just FOI

Environmental information is harder for a public body to withhold than an ordinary FOI. Use it to force out the Solent–airport handover Letter of Agreement, the airport's Noise and Track Keeping (NTK) radar logs for named flights, and its CDA compliance figures, which it does not publish.

Suggested: Avery + Sue

2Attack the redesign's "baseline"

The CAA has already told the airport its baseline scenario was weak. Get our independent data recognised as the true baseline, so a future claim of "no significant change" cannot be measured against a flattering starting point.

Suggested: Michael + Guy

3Get a protective "Design Principle" adopted

The CAP 1616 process lets communities shape the principles the new design must meet. Push for one that protects the National Park, for example no routine overflight of Brockenhurst or the NFNP below a set height. Avery has already found the Design Principles material.

Suggested: Avery + Russell

4Lobby the airlines directly

The airlines, not only the airport, choose how they descend. Use their own environmental pledges and the airport's own line that it "engages airline safety teams" to send each carrier a data-backed note on its worst Brockenhurst flights.

Suggested: Steve + Michael

5Runway-08 complaint alerts

Auto-detect from the flight data when the airport switches to easterly operations and ping the group, so complaints land in coordinated bursts on the worst days instead of trickling in one at a time.

Suggested: Michael

6Frame it as public health

World Health Organisation guidance puts recommended night-noise limits well below what residents are getting. Brief the council and Hampshire public-health teams and local GPs, and lean on the council's public-health duty, not just planning.

Suggested: Sue

7Build a cross-parish alliance

One village is easy to dismiss; a bloc is not. Federate with Sway, Burley and other affected communities plus the National Park Authority, and present to UKADS as a single voice, without diluting our own asks.

Suggested: John

8Get a preliminary legal opinion, crowdfunded

As Luton's group did, a short opinion on whether the airport is meeting its Section 106 "all reasonable endeavours" duty would sharpen every ask and could be crowdfunded across the affected villages.

Suggested: Dave + Avery
Also worth exploring: engage the airport's corporate owner and its published sustainability goals, above the operations team (owner to be confirmed); pursue a Deed of Variation to the Section 106 to create a Brockenhurst-claimable community noise fund and a penalty for low or off-course flights (as at some other airports); and publish a simple live "flights and complaints" dashboard on the website to keep momentum visible and give the press a hook.
