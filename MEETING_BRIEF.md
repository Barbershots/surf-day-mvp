# Brockenhurst overflight — working‑group briefing

A short, honest brief to help the group build a case to Bournemouth Airport:
**(1)** that there is a low‑flying problem over Brockenhurst, and **(2)** a
credible proposal to route arrivals to one side of the village, over open forest.

---

## Aim 1 — Show there is a problem (this part is strong)

We have three years of **public flight data** (2023–2025) taken from the
aircraft's **own GPS/ADS‑B broadcasts** — independent of the airport. Headlines:

- **Brockenhurst sits directly under the runway‑26 approach.** ~**76%** of
  airliner arrivals come in over the village (the rest land the other way, on
  runway 08, and miss it). The track map shows a clear hotspot straight through
  the village.
- **They're lower than a quiet descent would put them.** A proper "glide‑down"
  continuous descent would be ~**3,100 ft** over Brockenhurst. Airliners
  average ~**2,800 ft**, and **66% → 73% → 74%** (2023→2025) are **below** the
  quiet‑descent line — and the average is **falling** each year.
- **They level off low instead of gliding.** A continuous descent has no level
  flight. We can see arrivals **levelling off** over the village — the times a
  plane flies level it must add engine power, which is the noise. Level‑offs over
  the village rose **63 → 95 → 157** a year.
- **Nights are getting worse.** Night airliners over Brockenhurst rose
  **263 → 332 → 583**; those below the 2,000 ft minimum went **0 → 2 → 7**.
- **It's the airport's own standard we're measuring against.** Bournemouth's
  published rules (UK AIP, EGHH AD 2.21) require a *"continuous descent… at all
  times,"* staying *"as high an altitude as practical,"* joining the approach
  *"without level flight"* — and a **night rule** (2130–0630): establish no
  closer than **8 miles / not below 2,500 ft**.

**Bring to the meeting / the airport:** the 2‑page brief PDF, the year‑by‑year
descent graph, the night analytics dashboard, the track map, and the single
worked example (Jet2, 12 Oct 2025, levelling at ~2,050 ft over the village at
23:28). Everything is reproducible and sourced.

---

## Aim 2 — Propose an alternative route (frame this carefully)

The instinct — "route the planes north or south of the village, over open
forest" — is sound, **but it must be framed correctly or it will be dismissed.**
There are two separate levers; propose **both**, because one is quick and free.

### Lever A — Height (quick, cheap, no airspace change)
Ask the airport to **actually deliver the continuous descent its own rules
already require**: keep arrivals higher and descending steadily (on/above the
~3° path, ~3,000 ft over Brockenhurst) and **stop the low level‑offs**. This
needs **no airspace change** — it's ATC technique and airline engagement, using
the airport's *own* published policy. Higher + continuous = quieter over the
village, starting now.

### Lever B — Lateral routing (bigger, needs a formal process)
Key technical fact to get right: **the final ~5 miles cannot be moved** — it has
to line up with the runway (that's the ILS). **But Brockenhurst is ~10 miles
out**, and out there aircraft do **not** have to be on the straight extended
centreline. They can be routed to **join the final approach north *or* south of
the village**, over open forest, then turn onto the runway line closer in.
Modern satellite‑based approaches (RNAV/RNP) make such a "join after the village"
path precisely designable. Our track map already shows many aircraft join from
wider out — the ask is to make that the **standard**, on a defined corridor that
avoids the village.

**Be honest about these points (they protect your credibility):**
- You're **not** asking to move the runway or the final approach — only the
  *joining* path further out.
- A lateral change is a formal **CAA Airspace Change Proposal (process CAP1616)**,
  led by the airport / its air‑traffic provider (NATS). It takes time and
  includes public consultation — residents can **request it and must be
  consulted**.
- **Don't pre‑pick north vs south.** Each needs a check of *who lives
  underneath* and other constraints (Southampton's airspace to the north‑east
  may make one side easier). Ask the airport/NATS to **assess both** and choose
  the lower‑impact option — offer to help identify the quietest corridor.

## Likely airport push‑back → your answer

- *"It's standard / safe / constrained by Southampton airspace."* → Agreed there
  are constraints, but **Lever A needs no airspace change at all**, and lateral
  joins are routinely designed elsewhere within similar constraints.
- *"2,000–2,500 ft is a normal approach height."* → It's **below** their own
  continuous‑descent expectation; the real issue is **level flight**, and it's
  **getting worse**.
- *"You only capture some flights."* → **97%** of arrivals are tracked; the
  precise‑height sample is representative and unbiased, so our **counts are
  minimums** — the true numbers are higher.
- *"It's just wind / one‑offs."* → It's a **consistent, three‑year, worsening
  pattern**, not isolated flights.

## Who to approach & the process

- **Bournemouth Airport** — Environmental / Noise team, and the **Airport
  Consultative Committee** (community‑representation body). Ask for Brockenhurst
  representation, and for the airport's **own CDA‑compliance and WebTrak data**.
- **NATS / the airport's air‑traffic provider** — for procedure/airspace design.
- **CAA** — owns the **CAP1616** airspace‑change process (for Lever B).
- **New Forest National Park Authority** — a strong statutory ally (its purpose
  is to conserve the Park); overflight of a National Park is a powerful hook.
  Also the **parish/town council**, **local district council**, and the **MP**.

## Suggested concrete asks
1. Airport to **publish its runway‑26 CDA‑compliance** and commit to improving it
   (higher, continuous, no level‑offs) — near‑term, no airspace change.
2. Airport/NATS to run a **feasibility study of an RNAV join** that keeps
   runway‑26 arrivals north **or** south of Brockenhurst.
3. **Enforce the existing night rule** (2,500 ft / 8 miles) and review night
   movements.
4. **Brockenhurst seat** on the Airport Consultative Committee.
